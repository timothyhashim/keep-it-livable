Protect the places that keep us alive.

Clean air, fresh water, healthy soil and stable weather are not extras. Environmental protection is the work of keeping them intact, and most of that work is done by ordinary people making steady choices.

Four pressures worth understanding

Open each one for what is happening and what helps.

A warming climate

Burning coal, oil and gas adds carbon dioxide faster than forests and oceans can absorb it. The Paris Agreement set a goal of holding warming well below 2°C and pursuing 1.5°C, because each fraction of a degree brings more heat waves, floods and crop stress.

What helps: cleaner electricity, efficient buildings, public transport, and protecting forests and peatlands that store carbon.

Disappearing species and habitat

A 2019 global assessment by the UN’s biodiversity panel estimated that around one million animal and plant species face extinction, many within decades. The main drivers are changes to land and sea use, overexploitation, climate change, pollution and invasive species.

What helps: protected areas that connect, restoring wetlands and woodland, sustainable farming and fishing, and buying certified wood and seafood.

Pressure on fresh water

Rivers, lakes and aquifers supply drinking water and irrigate crops, and they are also where untreated waste, fertilizer runoff and industrial pollution end up. Healthy wetlands filter water and soften floods, so losing them costs communities twice.

What helps: safe sanitation, protecting wetlands and river banks, fixing leaks, and keeping chemicals and oils out of drains.

Waste and plastic pollution

Research published in 2015 estimated millions of tonnes of plastic enter the ocean every year. The UN food agency estimates that roughly a third of food produced is lost or wasted, which also wastes the water, land and energy used to grow it.

What helps: reducing single-use items, repairing and reusing, composting food scraps, and recycling what is left.
